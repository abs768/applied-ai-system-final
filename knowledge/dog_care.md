## Exercise and walks
Most adult dogs need 30 to 120 minutes of activity a day, depending on breed and energy level. High-energy and
working breeds (border collies, huskies, retrievers) need the upper end; small or brachycephalic breeds (pugs,
bulldogs) do better with shorter, cooler walks. Split exercise into at least two sessions so the dog is not
under-stimulated for long stretches. Morning walks before work help with house-training and calm behavior.

## Senior dogs
Dogs over roughly 7 years (earlier for giant breeds) benefit from shorter, more frequent, low-impact walks rather
than one long outing. Watch for stiffness, heavy panting, or reluctance to climb stairs and mention them to a vet.
Senior dogs often need an extra short toilet break in the evening.

TASK: Evening toilet break | category=walk | minutes=10 | priority=medium | time=21:30 | frequency=daily

## Puppies
Puppies need toilet breaks after waking, after eating, and after play, roughly every 2 hours while awake when
young. Keep structured exercise short (a common rule of thumb is about 5 minutes per month of age, up to twice a
day) to protect growing joints. Short training sessions of 5 to 10 minutes work better than long ones.

## Feeding
Adult dogs are usually fed twice a day at consistent times, about 8 to 12 hours apart. Puppies under 6 months
are usually fed three times a day. Always provide fresh water and wash the bowl daily. Measure portions using
the food label and the dog's body condition rather than free-feeding.

## Enrichment and training
Mental stimulation is as important as physical exercise. Puzzle feeders, sniff walks, and 10-minute training
sessions reduce boredom behaviors like chewing and barking. Rotate toys weekly to keep them interesting.
TASK: Puzzle feeder or sniff game | category=enrichment | minutes=15 | priority=medium | frequency=daily

## Grooming
Brush short-coated dogs weekly and long or double-coated dogs several times a week. Check ears weekly for
redness or odor, and trim nails roughly every 3 to 4 weeks. Brushing teeth daily or several times a week helps
prevent dental disease.

TASK: Tooth brushing | category=grooming | minutes=5 | priority=medium | frequency=daily

## Hot weather
In hot weather, walk dogs early in the morning or late in the evening and avoid hot pavement; if the ground is
too hot for the back of your hand for 5 seconds, it is too hot for paws. Never leave a dog in a parked car.
Brachycephalic breeds overheat quickly and need extra care.
