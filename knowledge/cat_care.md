## Feeding
Adult cats do well with two or more measured meals a day; many cats prefer several small meals. Kittens under
6 months usually eat three to four times a day. Wet food helps hydration. Keep food and water away from the
litter box, and refresh water daily; many cats drink more from a fountain.

## Litter box
Scoop the litter box at least once a day and fully change the litter and wash the box about once a week. A common
guideline is one box per cat plus one extra. A sudden change in litter habits, straining, or urinating outside
the box can signal a medical problem; a male cat straining without producing urine is an emergency.

## Play and enrichment
Indoor cats need daily play to stay fit and avoid boredom: two or more short sessions of 10 to 15 minutes with a
wand toy work well, ideally ending with a food reward to mimic the hunt-catch-eat cycle. Window perches, puzzle
feeders, and scratching posts add enrichment.

## Grooming
Short-haired cats usually need brushing about once a week; long-haired cats (Persians, Maine Coons) need brushing
several times a week to prevent mats and hairballs. Trim nails every 2 to 3 weeks if the cat allows it.
TASK: Brush coat | category=grooming | minutes=10 | priority=medium | frequency=weekly

## Senior cats
Cats over about 10 years benefit from vet checkups every 6 months, easy-access litter boxes with low sides, and
watching for changes in appetite, thirst, weight, or litter habits, which can indicate kidney disease, diabetes,
or hyperthyroidism.
