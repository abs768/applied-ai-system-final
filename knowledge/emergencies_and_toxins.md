## When to call a vet immediately
Contact a veterinarian or an emergency animal hospital right away for: difficulty breathing, collapse, seizures,
uncontrolled bleeding, suspected poisoning, a swollen hard belly with retching (possible bloat in dogs), a male cat
straining to urinate, repeated vomiting, or a pet that has stopped eating (urgent for rabbits). In the US, the ASPCA
Animal Poison Control Center (888-426-4435) and Pet Poison Helpline (855-764-7661) can help with poisonings; fees
may apply.

## Common toxic foods
Foods that are toxic to dogs and cats include chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions, garlic and chives, xylitol
(found in sugar-free gum and some peanut butters), alcohol, caffeine, macadamia nuts (dogs), and raw yeast dough.
Lilies are extremely toxic to cats; even pollen or vase water can cause kidney failure.

## What PawPal+ does in an emergency
PawPal+ does not diagnose or treat. If a message describes a possible emergency, it stops normal planning, tells
the owner to contact a vet or poison hotline now, and does not suggest home remedies such as inducing vomiting.
