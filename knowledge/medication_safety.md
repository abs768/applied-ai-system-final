## Giving medication on time
Medications should be given at consistent times exactly as the veterinarian prescribed. Twice-daily ("BID")
medications are usually given about 12 hours apart, for example 08:00 and 20:00. Medication tasks should always
be treated as high priority so they are never dropped from a busy day. If a dose is missed, follow the vet's or
the label's instructions rather than doubling up.

TASK: Give medication (evening dose) | category=meds | minutes=5 | priority=high | time=20:00 | frequency=daily

## Insulin and diabetic pets
Diabetic dogs and cats usually receive insulin twice a day, about 12 hours apart, together with or right after a
meal, as directed by the vet. Feeding and insulin times should be paired and consistent. Signs of low blood sugar
(weakness, wobbling, seizures) are an emergency.
TASK: Meal + insulin (morning) | category=meds | minutes=10 | priority=high | time=07:30 | frequency=daily
TASK: Meal + insulin (evening) | category=meds | minutes=10 | priority=high | time=19:30 | frequency=daily

## Dosing questions
PawPal+ never recommends a dose, a drug, or a change in dose. Doses depend on the animal's weight, health, and the
specific product, and only a veterinarian can set them. Never give human medications such as ibuprofen,
acetaminophen (paracetamol), or naproxen to pets; they can be toxic, and acetaminophen is especially dangerous to
cats.

## Parasite prevention
Flea, tick, and heartworm preventives are usually given monthly. A monthly reminder task helps owners stay
consistent; the product and dose must come from the vet.
